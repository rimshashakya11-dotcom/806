New Zealand Airbnb Listings: BI Dashboard

Star-schema warehouse built from InsideAirbnb-style listing export (11,708 listings) joined to the official NZ ward/territorial-authority reference table (239 wards nationwide). Listings in this snapshot are concentrated in Auckland, Thames-Coromandel and Waikato (27 wards with real listings) – the ranking, map, location-lookup and compare tools below reflect only those wards. Snapshot as at last scrape.

About this dashboard: how it differs from booking.com, what it uses to price, privacy & security

What this is, versus booking.com. booking.com is a live marketplace: it shows real-time availability and lets you actually reserve a room. This is a business-intelligence and analytics tool built on top of a data warehouse: a snapshot of 11,708 real NZ Airbnb listings, star-schema modelled and joined to the official NZ ward reference data. It does not book anything and it does not show live inventory. What it does instead: it lets a host, property manager or analyst benchmark a listing against the market (the estimator and price-check tools below), spot over/under-priced properties, see where demand and price are concentrated (the ward map), and sanity-check their own portfolio against warehouse aggregates (the CSV upload tool). That is the real, practical use case: decision support for pricing and market analysis, not a booking flow.

What the price estimate is based on. The regression behind the estimator uses seven inputs together, not just bedrooms/bathrooms/beds: accommodates, bedrooms, bathrooms, beds, amenities count, room type, and ward (location). Ward and room type carry the largest weight because location and privacy level are the two strongest real-world price drivers in this dataset. It is fitted on all 11,708 listings (R² = 0.50, meaning roughly half of price variation is explained by these seven factors; the rest is noise this model cannot see — things like interior photos/staging quality, exact street or view, host responsiveness, real-time supply and demand, and day-of-week/event pricing). It is a benchmark, not a live Airbnb "Smart Pricing" algorithm, and it will not exactly match what Airbnb itself would suggest.

Login and privacy. The login on this dashboard is a UI demonstration only (see the sign-in screen for why), not real access control. Once past it, every tool below runs entirely in your browser: nothing you type or upload is ever sent to a server, stored, or tracked. The "Reset" and "Clear upload" buttons instantly wipe whatever you entered, and logging out reloads the page so every tool returns to its starting state.

Why there are no real Airbnb photos. This dashboard cannot load live images from Airbnb's own servers (the sandboxed environment it is built and published in blocks requests to unlisted external image hosts, and Airbnb's photo CDN is not reachable from here even for one-off downloads). Rather than fake or placeholder stock photos pretending to be real listings, the "Real sample listings" gallery below shows genuine listing data (name, price, room type, ward, rating, review count) with an honest generated colour treatment instead of a photograph, and says so explicitly.

Data storage & management: method comparison EVALUATION

Two alternative ways this warehouse could have been stored, evaluated against the approach actually used, on the criteria that matter for this business scenario.

CriterionPlain relational DB (OLTP-style)Cloud data warehouse / lakeRelational DB + star-schema warehouse (chosen)
Query performance for analyticsWeak – normalised tables need many joins per reportStrong – built for large-scale aggregationStrong – fact/dimension model is purpose-built for the ward/price/room-type roll-ups this dashboard runs
Cost at this data volume (~12k rows)LowHigher – pay for managed infrastructure not needed yetLow – a single-node relational engine easily holds this warehouse
Data integrity controlsStrong – native foreign keys & constraintsWeaker by default – schema-on-read, constraints often bolted onStrong – foreign-key constraints enforced at load, checked by the ETL (0 orphaned keys)
Security & access controlMature, well-understood role/grant modelStrong, but adds a new identity & access surface to manageMature relational role/grant model, no extra infrastructure to secure
Scalability if this grows nationwideLimited – normalised OLTP schema not designed for heavy analyticsVery highModerate – adequate for a nationwide snapshot, would need re-evaluation if this became a live, continuously-updated feed

Recommendation: for a project of this size and analytics-first purpose, a relational database holding a star-schema warehouse gives the best balance of low cost, strong integrity controls, and analytics-friendly querying. A cloud data warehouse becomes the better choice only if the data volume grows by an order of magnitude or the warehouse needs to ingest a continuous, live feed rather than periodic snapshots.

Best wards to invest in RANKED

Every ward ranked by realistic annual revenue potential (modelled price × occupancy × 365 nights) for an entire home/apt, alongside how many listings already compete there. Use this to shortlist areas before digging into the detailed tools above.

"Competition" is simply how many listings already exist in that ward: a high number can mean strong tourist demand, or a saturated market, so read it alongside revenue potential rather than alone.

Superhost price premium BY WARD

Is "become a superhost" actually worth it where you operate? Pick a ward to see the real median price superhosts vs. non-superhosts charge there. Wards with too few listings to split reliably aren't shown.

Amenities worth adding DESCRIPTIVE, NOT CAUSAL

The amenities most associated with higher-priced listings in this warehouse, ranked by the percentage price gap between listings that have each amenity and those that don't. This is a correlation, not proof the amenity itself causes the higher price: it's also picking up that these amenities are more common in larger, pricier entire-home listings, so treat it as a signal worth investigating, not a guarantee.
