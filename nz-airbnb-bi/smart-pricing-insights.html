Recommendations

What the host-tools team should build and test

  1. 1Ship a "Pricing Insights" panel, host-facing only, showing their under/well/overpriced category and the two or three attributes driving it. No automatic price changes — the host decides.
  2. 2Prioritise the underpriced segment first. It's 39.7% of the market and the lowest-risk revenue source — nobody loses bookings by finding out they can charge more.
  3. 3Validate with an A/B test before wide rollout — a held-out control group, checked for booking volume, revenue and host satisfaction, not just the model's own accuracy.
  4. 4Add time-series booking data in a future iteration for genuine day-of-week and seasonal pricing, and photo/listing-completeness quality signals if they become available.
  5. 5Run a quarterly fairness audit comparing recommended changes across regions and property types, with a human in the loop before any suggestion becomes a pre-filled default.

Limitations

The model predicts what the market currently pays for similar listings — it does not prove that raising an underpriced listing's rate will causally produce that price at the same occupancy. It also can't see genuine quality signals (photos, furnishing standard, competitive dynamics), which is a real share of the ~$103 average error.

  • Cross-sectional data only — no seasonality, events, or day-of-week effects
  • Trained on the 1st–99th price percentile ($53–$1,919) — not validated at the extremes
  • The $21.5m figure is illustrative, not causal — actual uplift depends on price elasticity, not modelled here
  • Specific to New Zealand's market structure — reusable elsewhere only with local retraining
